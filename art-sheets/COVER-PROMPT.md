# Cover art — the portal thumbnail prompt

Hand-written. **Not** generated by `pnpm art:prompts` (that only writes
`PROMPTS-RUNES/BOARD/CAST/SINGLES.md` + `PAINT-STATUS.md`), so edits here survive.

This is the one image a player judges Glyphyx by: on CrazyGames / Poki / GamePix /
GameMonetize it is shown as a small tile in a wall of other tiles. It has about half a
second and roughly **200 x 112 px** to say *"tactical rune duel, and something just
exploded."* Everything below is engineered for that, not for looking good at full size.

## The five rules the prompt encodes

1. **One read, not four.** A single brightest object (the falling rune stone) that a
   squint test still finds at 200 px. Everything else is context, in shadow.
2. **A face, lit, with an emotion.** Faces are the highest-CTR element in a portal grid.
3. **A verb in the picture.** The player must see *what you do*: a stone being dropped
   onto a board, and something shattering because of it.
4. **Colour conflict.** Teal player side against red-magenta enemy side, on a dark
   indigo ground, so the tile pops out of the portal's own dark UI.
5. **No text.** Portals print the title themselves, and baked-in wordmarks either get
   rejected or read as an ad. The title lockup is a separate variant (D).

## How to run it

1. Open Gemini (Nano-Banana / Gemini image generation) and **attach the on-model
   references**, so the runes and the cast come back as *this* game rather than generic
   fantasy art:
   - `art-sheets/painted/sheet-runes-melee.jpg` — stone materials, glyph, gold Lv 2 rim
   - `art-sheets/painted/sheet-tiles.jpg` — the board tiles and their owner glows
   - `art-sheets/painted/walk-teal.jpg` — the player's rune-keeper (teal cloak, satchel)
   - `art-sheets/painted/walk-snaggletusk.jpg` — the orc, for the enemy across the board
   - `art-sheets/painted/bg-sky.jpg` + `bg-ridge-near.jpg` — the night sky and monoliths
2. Paste **Prompt A** below. Ask for 16:9.
3. Generate **at least six**. Judge them ONLY as 200 px thumbnails (shrink them, or view
   the folder at small-icon size). Discard anything that needs zooming to work.
4. Upscale the keeper to **1600 x 900**, export it, and put the folder through
   `pnpm compress-folder <dir>` — the local compressor picks the lowest quality each
   file survives (see the root README's Dev tools). Cut the square and portrait sizes
   from variants B / C — *re-generate* them, do not centre-crop the 16:9; a crop loses
   the hero's face every time.
5. If you need a wordmark: `public/images/logo/*` is still the **Survivalist** logo from
   the base project. It is the wrong name for this game and has to be redrawn before any
   store page goes out.

## Prompt A — master 16:9 cover  (attach the references above)

```text
ONE FINISHED ILLUSTRATION — a key-art cover for a fantasy strategy game.
Landscape 16:9, 1600 x 900 pixels. Not a sprite sheet, not a grid of panels,
not a UI mockup, not a screenshot. One scene, painted edge to edge.

THE SUBJECT: a duel fought by dropping carved rune stones onto a small stone
board. A young rune-keeper has just hurled a glowing rune down onto the board
and it is landing, right now, in a burst of light — while an orc warlord leans
in from the far side and one of his stones bursts into rubble.

READING ORDER — obey this hierarchy exactly; it matters more than any detail.
The picture must survive being shrunk to 200 pixels wide.
1. BRIGHTEST AND BIGGEST — a single large rune stone in mid-air, low centre-right,
   caught a hand's width above the board, blazing. It is a smooth dark pebble with
   a crimson SWORD glyph carved into it, the groove lit from inside (#ff3b4a), a
   polished gold rim around it and a small gold crest on its shoulder marking it as
   upgraded. A hard ring of crimson light and dust blows outward from under it. It
   is the first thing the eye lands on and it fills roughly a quarter of the image
   height.
2. SECOND — the rune-keeper's FACE, lower left, turned toward the camera, lit from
   below and from the right by that crimson flash: a young keeper in a hooded teal
   cloak with a satchel of stones, throwing arm still extended toward the board
   after the release. Eyes wide, teeth showing, a fierce delighted grin — the
   expression of someone who knows this move just won. The face reads clearly and
   is not in shadow.
3. THIRD — the BOARD: a 4 x 4 grid of thick slate tiles (#23283a) seen from a low
   three-quarter angle, filling the bottom third and running out of frame at the
   bottom edge. The near tiles glow teal-blue in their bevels (#4fd6ff) because the
   keeper holds them; the far tiles glow hot red-magenta (#ff4d5a); a faint violet
   edge light (#8a5cff) runs along every seam. Six or seven rune stones already
   stand on it, small and clearly separate: an emerald bow stone, a sapphire shield
   stone and an amethyst orb stone on the near side, crimson-lit enemy stones on the
   far side. One enemy stone at the far right is SHATTERING — split into three or
   four chunks of rubble flying apart with sparks, its glyph light dying.
4. FOURTH, and dark — the ORC WARLORD across the board, upper right, leaning in over
   it with a snagged tusk and a snarl: mostly a heavy dark silhouette with a hot
   red-magenta rim light down his edge, cropped by the right frame. He is a threat
   and a shape, never a second focal point; his face is smaller and dimmer than the
   keeper's.
5. LAST, almost black — the night behind them: deep indigo sky going near-black at
   the top, a small pale moon high on the right with a soft halo, a drift of violet
   aurora, and a low ridge of broken black rock carrying two standing rune monoliths
   in silhouette, their carvings faintly violet. Hazy, out of focus, quiet.

MOTION — two thin accents only, both crossing behind the airborne stone and both
dim next to it: one emerald arrow streak (#35e07a) skimming low across the board,
and one violet diagonal beam (#b57bff) cutting through the far corner. No more than
these two. Dust motes and a few embers ride the impact light.

STAGING AND CROP SAFETY — the thrown stone and the keeper's face both sit INSIDE the
middle square of the image (this picture gets cropped to a square and to a tall
portrait for some stores, so nothing that matters may live in the outer eighth on any
side). Do not centre the composition dead-on: the diagonal runs from the keeper at
lower left, through the falling stone, up to the orc at upper right.

CONTRAST — a dark image with a small, very bright core. Roughly three quarters of the
canvas is deep indigo, black-brown and slate; saturated colour is confined to the
glyph glows, the impact burst and the two tile glows. The thumbnail must POP against
a dark website background.

STYLE — hand-drawn stylised MYTHICAL FANTASY game art, the same hand as a painted
board-game piece or a plate from an illuminated bestiary:
· INK FIRST. Confident dark warm-umber contour lines of varying weight, still visible
  through the paint. It should look DRAWN, not rendered.
· PAINTERLY gouache and watercolour washes inside the lines, visible brush texture,
  softly mottled, volume from ONE key light plus the crimson flash. No airbrush.
· CHUNKY, READABLE SILHOUETTES — every element is one strong shape; small detail is
  suggested with a few confident marks, never fussed over.
· PALETTE: warm sandstone, ochre, umber and slate, earthy and slightly desaturated,
  with the arcane glows (#ff3b4a crimson, #35e07a emerald, #b57bff amethyst, #4aa8ff
  sapphire, #ffd23f topaz, #8a5cff violet) and polished gold (#ffefb0 through #e6b53a
  to #a8741a) as the ONLY saturated notes.
· Mythic and weathered: chipped edges, hairline cracks, dust in the grooves. Charming
  craft rather than slick rendering.

AVOID — this is exactly how earlier attempts went wrong:
· NO text, letters, numbers, wordmarks, logos, titles, captions, watermarks,
  signatures, rating badges, store buttons or "PLAY NOW" flashes. Not one glyph of
  writing anywhere, including on banners, monoliths and stone faces.
· NO user interface: no health bars, no score, no hand of cards along the bottom, no
  timer, no buttons, no cursor, no phone or tablet frame around the picture.
· NO frame, border, vignette, matting, paper edge or drop-shadowed card. The painting
  runs to all four edges.
· NO glossy plastic mobile-game look, no bevelled 3D chrome, no lens flares, no bloom
  haze over the whole picture, no photorealism, no flat vector icon look.
· NO heavy uniform black outlines and no hard cel-shaded banding.
· NO collage of separate vignettes, no split screen, no comic panels, no before/after.
· NO extra heroes, armies, crowds, castles, dragons or horses. Two characters, one
  board, and nothing else with a face.
· Do not tilt the board so far that it becomes a wall, and do not draw it from directly
  above — it is a low three-quarter table view.

OUTPUT: one image, 1600 x 900 pixels, 16:9 landscape, full bleed, no border.
```

## Variant B — square 1:1 (store icon / grid tile)

Same prompt with these overrides pasted at the end. **Re-generate; never crop A.**

```text
OVERRIDES FOR THIS VERSION:
· OUTPUT: one image, 1024 x 1024 pixels, square 1:1.
· Tighter framing: the falling crimson sword rune stone now fills roughly a THIRD of
  the image height, centre-right; the keeper's lit face is bottom-left and larger; the
  board is only its top two ranks, running out of the bottom edge.
· Reduce the orc to a dark shoulder-and-tusk shape in the top right corner, and drop
  the moon, the aurora and the ridge entirely — plain deep indigo sky behind them.
· Keep the emerald arrow streak; remove the violet beam.
```

## Variant C — portrait 4:5 / 9:16 (vertical placements, ad units)

```text
OVERRIDES FOR THIS VERSION:
· OUTPUT: one image, 1080 x 1350 pixels, portrait 4:5.  (Or 1080 x 1920, 9:16.)
· Stack the diagonal vertically: the orc's dark leaning silhouette across the TOP
  quarter, the falling crimson rune stone blazing in the MIDDLE, the board and the
  shattering enemy stone below it, and the keeper's lit upturned face in the BOTTOM
  left corner, looking up at the stone.
· Leave the top eighth and the bottom eighth visually quiet — they get overlaid.
```

## Variant D — with a title lockup (only where a portal wants one)

Prefer setting the title as real type over variant A in an image editor. If it must be
painted, append:

```text
OVERRIDES FOR THIS VERSION:
· Add ONE piece of lettering and nothing else: the single word "GLYPHYX", in the upper
  left quadrant, carved into weathered stone slabs — chunky angular capitals, chipped
  edges, warm sandstone, a thin crimson glyph-glow in the carved grooves and a polished
  gold edge highlight. Spelled exactly G-L-Y-P-H-Y-X, seven letters, on one line, level
  and unrotated.
· Nothing else in the picture carries writing of any kind.
· Keep the word clear of the keeper's face and of the falling stone.
```

Gemini mangles lettering often — check the spelling letter by letter before keeping one.

## A/B the concept, not just the render

CTR is measured, not designed. Generate all three and look at each for half a second:

| | Concept | Why it might win |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | *The Slam* (above) — hero throws, stone lands, orc reacts | Verb + face + stakes; the safe favourite |
| **A2** | *The Stare-down* — keeper and orc facing each other over a glowing board, both faces lit from below by it, the board mid-explosion between them | Two faces, instant "duel"; reads as strategy faster |
| **A3** | *The Macro* — one huge upgraded rune stone almost filling the frame, gold-rimmed and blazing, the tiny board and two tiny figures reflected in its polished face | Strongest pure shape at 200 px; weakest at explaining the genre |

For A2 / A3, keep every STYLE, AVOID, CONTRAST and CROP SAFETY block from prompt A
verbatim and swap only the READING ORDER block.

## Before you ship one

- [ ] Shrink to 200 x 112 — is there still one obvious bright focal point?
- [ ] Centre-crop it to a square — is the keeper's face still in frame?
- [ ] Zero letters anywhere (check monoliths, banners, stone faces, the satchel).
- [ ] No UI, no frame, no border; full bleed to all four edges.
- [ ] The board still reads as a **4 x 4 grid of separate tiles** — not 5 x 5, not a
      chessboard, not a wall. The grid *is* the genre promise.
- [ ] Stones match the painted sheets (same materials, same gold Lv 2 rim), so the cover
      does not promise art the game does not have.
- [ ] Exported at 1600 x 900, compressed, and the file is not 3 MB.
